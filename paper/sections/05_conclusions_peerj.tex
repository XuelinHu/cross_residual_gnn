\section*{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies CR-GNN as a controlled biomolecular graph-classification framework rather than as a claim of universal model superiority. The final evidence supports a narrower but stronger conclusion: cross-residual interaction is useful, but residual variants remain the strongest default family in the reported benchmark suite, and the gains are conditional on both dataset regime and routing strategy.

The main contribution is therefore not a universally dominant architecture, but a controlled comparison of information-flow mechanisms and residual-routing strategies that reveals when and how residual routing helps. Across the biological core, GraphResGNN leads numerically on PROTEINS and ENZYMES through tail-preserving residual routing---repeated reinjection of graph-level summaries---while NodeResGNN leads on DD through node-level historical structural signal preservation. Cross-residual variants provide selective gains on MUTAG and Mutagenicity, where rare discriminative motif preservation via complementary parallel pathways matters most, but they do not replace residual baselines as the main point of reference. Importantly, most numerical gaps between architectures are modest relative to fold-level variation; only a subset of comparisons reach $p<0.05$ under paired statistical tests, reinforcing the paper's bounded interpretation.

The routing analysis sharpens that result further. Learnable dense routing remains strongest on the best graph-level residual line, while mild sparse routing (selective residual filtering) is frequently effective on cross-oriented settings. This shows that the practical question is not only whether an additional residual path exists, but how historical information is filtered before reuse. The targeted routing results therefore help explain why the benchmark hierarchy is mixed rather than one-sided: the benefit of an extra reuse path depends on whether it preserves task-relevant structure or introduces head-pattern dominance through unfiltered signal accumulation.

The scope of this conclusion should remain disciplined. The strongest claims in the paper are supported by the stratified five-fold benchmark and the targeted five-fold ablations, whereas the older sensitivity scans are only supportive. The statistical evidence (Table~\ref{tab:statistical_tests}) is informative precisely because it confirms that architecture-level effects are modest under the controlled protocol---a pattern that aligns with the paper's mechanism-oriented rather than leaderboard-oriented framing. Likewise, the present study addresses controlled graph-classification behavior on medium-scale biomolecular and molecular benchmarks; it does not by itself establish large-scale scalability or direct biological interpretability.

Taken together, these results position CR-GNN as a practical biomolecular graph-classification design space and provide a methodological basis for future work on richer biological inputs, larger benchmarks, and graph-learning settings where residual reuse must be coordinated with stronger domain supervision.
