\begin{abstract}
Deep graph classification benefits from residual information flow, but the exact value of cross-branch interaction depends strongly on how node-level and graph-level signals are coupled in the implementation. This paper studies Enhanced Cross Residual Graph Neural Networks (ECR-GNN), a modular family of six architectures built from three message-passing operators (GCNConv, GATConv, and TransformerConv) and two reusable residual mechanisms: intra-branch residual accumulation and cross-branch residual exchange. Rather than describing the framework abstractly, we align the method section with the executed PyTorch Geometric implementation and derive the experimental conclusions directly from the raw record exports under \texttt{records/}. We verify 24 full v3 record files (720 configurations) and incorporate a newer v4 rerun that replaces the older MUTAG+GCNConv slice with 60 updated measurements. Across the merged benchmark, ResBlockGnn achieves the best overall mean accuracy (0.775), CrossGraphBlockGnn attains the strongest AIDS result (0.815), and residual variants consistently improve depth tolerance over the plain BlockGNN baseline. The updated MUTAG+GCNConv rerun further reveals a more nuanced picture: graph-level residual passing is strongest at shallow depth on this slice, whereas ResBlockGnn remains the most stable architecture overall. These results suggest that residual design matters more reliably than simply increasing branch count, and that cross-branch fusion is most useful when matched to dataset scale and operator bias.
\end{abstract}
