\begin{abstract}
Deep biomolecular graph learning remains difficult because repeated message passing can wash out local structural cues while simple identity skips do not explicitly model complementary pathways. This limitation matters not only for protein-related graph classification, but also for biological network inference settings where local motifs and global relational context must be preserved together, including emerging plant-oriented graph learning pipelines for protein identification and functional annotation. This paper studies Cross-Residual Graph Neural Networks (CR-GNN), a compact family of graph architectures that reuses intermediate states either within a branch, across parallel branches, or through graph-level summaries passed between sequential blocks. Rather than claiming that every cross-residual design is universally superior, we ask when cross-path information exchange is useful relative to both plain stacking and stronger residual reuse. We evaluate plain, residual, and cross-residual variants under a unified training and analysis workflow with validation-based early stopping, learning-rate scheduling, gradient clipping, and TensorBoard-backed diagnostics. The main biological package centers on the protein-oriented TU benchmarks PROTEINS, DD, and ENZYMES, while MUTAG, AIDS, and Mutagenicity are retained only as supplementary robustness checks. The empirical picture remains selective rather than absolute: residual variants stay the strongest default family, whereas cross-residual variants provide dataset-dependent gains and repeatedly improve over the plain baseline. Overall, CR-GNN is best understood as a protein-oriented biomolecular graph learning backbone with a credible extension path toward plant-related graph inference and future omics-enriched settings, rather than as a completed integrative-omics system.
\end{abstract}
