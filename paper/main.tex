\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks are increasingly used in biomolecular and plant-related graph learning, yet deep message passing can blur local structural cues while simple skip connections do not test richer information reuse. This limitation is especially relevant for biological graph classification, where predictive signals often depend on both local motifs and graph-level context. We therefore study Cross-Residual Graph Neural Networks (CR-GNN), a compact model family that compares five information-flow patterns under a shared backbone: PlainGNN, NodeResGNN, NodeCrossGNN, GraphResGNN, and GraphCrossGNN. Experiments on a protein-oriented benchmark centered on PROTEINS, DD, and ENZYMES, with MUTAG, AIDS, and Mutagenicity as supplementary robustness checks, show a consistent picture. Residual reuse is the strongest default strategy, while cross-residual routing usually improves over plain stacking and delivers dataset-dependent gains on selected graphs. These results position CR-GNN as a practical biomolecular graph representation framework and clarify when cross-path interaction is useful in biologically motivated graph learning.
\end{abstract}
