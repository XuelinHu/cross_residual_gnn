\section*{Introduction}

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are now a standard approach for learning from molecular and protein graphs because they encode local neighborhood structure together with higher-order relational context \cite{kipf2016semi,gilmer2017neural,hamilton2017inductive}. In biomolecular graph classification, however, deeper message passing is not automatically beneficial. Repeated propagation can smooth away informative intermediate states, and conventional skip connections only test same-path reuse rather than richer alternatives for preserving historical information.

This paper studies Cross-Residual Graph Neural Networks (CR-GNN), a controlled model family that compares plain stacking, node-level residual reuse, node-level cross-residual exchange, graph-level residual propagation, and graph-level cross-summary exchange under shared graph-classification backbones. The central question is deliberately narrower than a generic model race: when does cross-residual interaction help on biomolecular graph classification, when does residual reuse remain the stronger default, and how does residual routing design change that answer?

The work is organized as a biomolecular graph-classification methods study rather than as a direct biological discovery paper. The main evidence comes from a protein-oriented benchmark core built around PROTEINS, DD, and ENZYMES, together with supplementary molecular robustness datasets. This design keeps the paper close to biologically meaningful graph settings while maintaining a reproducible benchmark scope. The paper first motivates the architectural question, then presents the methodological setting, the benchmark results, the targeted gate and routing ablations, and the supporting sensitivity studies.

\subsection*{Contributions}

The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize CR-GNN as a compact architecture family that compares five graph-classification variants under one controlled framework: PlainGNN, NodeResGNN, NodeCrossGNN, GraphResGNN, and GraphCrossGNN.
    \item We extend the study beyond architecture comparison by introducing residual-routing analysis through learnable, top-k, and sparse routing modes.
    \item We organize the final empirical study around a unified \texttt{V3} benchmark with six datasets, nine methods, four operators, and stratified five-fold evaluation, supplemented by targeted gate and routing ablations.
    \item We show that cross-residual interaction is useful but conditional: residual reuse remains the stronger default family, while mild sparse routing is often advantageous on selected cross-oriented settings.
\end{itemize}
