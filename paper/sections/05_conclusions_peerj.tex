\section*{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper reframed the cross-residual GNN study around biomolecular graph representation learning rather than generic benchmark breadth. The final result is a narrower but stronger claim: cross-residual interaction is useful, yet residual variants remain the strongest default family in the reported benchmark suite.

The main contribution is therefore not a universally dominant model, but a controlled architecture family that makes the trade-offs among plain stacking, residual reuse, and cross-residual interaction directly visible. Across the protein-oriented benchmark core, GraphResGNN is strongest on PROTEINS and ENZYMES, whereas NodeResGNN is strongest on DD. Cross-residual variants still provide useful gains over plain stacking, especially on ENZYMES and selected supplementary datasets, but they do not replace residual baselines as the default point of reference.

These results position CR-GNN as a practical biomolecular graph representation framework and provide a credible methodological basis for future work on richer protein and plant-related graph inference settings.
